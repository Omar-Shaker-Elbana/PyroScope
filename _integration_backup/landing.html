the challenge

Same planet, different instruments

MODIS has flown since 1999. VIIRS took over the watch from 2011 and sees fires at a finer scale. Put their records side by side and fire counts jump, not because the world changed but because the sensor did.

The challenge asks for a way to make the two agree. PyroScope is our answer, and it comes with a map you can click.

harmonization

Many detections in, one record out

Counting raw pixels would favor VIIRS, whose pixels are smaller. We count unique active grid cells per day instead, then use the years when both sensors flew to learn how one maps onto the other.

the method

Four steps from raw detections

  1. Filter

    Drop low-confidence detections before anything is counted.

  2. Grid

    Snap both sensors to the same cells of about 2 km, one UTC day at a time.

  3. Calibrate

    Use the overlap years to scale each sensor to the union of active cells, and VIIRS FRP to a MODIS-equivalent scale.

  4. Explore

    Browse calendars, unusual days and critical burning windows for any spot on land.
