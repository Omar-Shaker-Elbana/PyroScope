# PyroScope methodology, validation and known limits

All numbers below come from the Egypt 2015-01-02 to 2025-12-31 standard-processing files used by the author (not included in the repository; see README, "Data in this repository")
(MODIS Terra + Aqua, VIIRS S-NPP 375 m), run with the default settings. Every one of them is regenerated by
`python -m src.harmonize` and `python -m src.anomaly` and can be re-read from `frp_mapping.json`,
`grid_sensitivity.csv`, `anomaly_validation.json` and `anomaly_events.csv`.

| Review item | Where it is addressed |
|---|---|
| A. The harmonization factor is not stable enough; Aqua and Terra must be separated | section 1 |
| B. A day with no detections is not a day without observations | section 2 |
| C. The anomaly detection needs validation | section 3 |
| D. The 0.5 degree grid needs justification and a sensitivity analysis | section 4 |

## 1. The FRP scale k

### 1.1 What k is, and is not

`k = sum(VIIRS FRP inside MODIS footprints) / sum(MODIS FRP)`, computed over MODIS pixels that contain at least one
VIIRS 375 m detection, with the two overpasses at most 30 minutes apart (`PYRO_K_MAX_DT_MIN`). It is

* a ratio of **totals**, so it is dominated by the large fires, and it does not describe a typical pixel (the median
  of pixel ratios is 0.74 and the geometric mean 0.67, against 0.82 for the ratio of sums);
* **conditional on MODIS having detected the footprint**: it is not a detection-probability correction, and it says
  nothing about the many small fires only VIIRS sees;
* specific to **one MODIS satellite**: every satellite has its own orbit, overpass time and calibration history.

### 1.2 Aqua and Terra are separate

`fit_scale` is run once per MODIS satellite, each against the VIIRS reference satellite.

| | matched pixels within 30 min of S-NPP | k | status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Aqua | 1,864 | 0.821 (95% CI 0.739-0.905) | fitted; trust MEDIUM |
| Terra | 3 (median gap to S-NPP over all 1,288 pixel matches: 149 min) | none | NOT_VALIDATED |

Terra crosses Egypt at about 10:30 local solar time and S-NPP at about 13:30, so a Terra pixel and an S-NPP pixel in
the same place are three hours apart and see different fire states (the diurnal cycle of fires). A ratio computed
over those pairs is not a calibration. It is reported only as `k_diagnostic_all_gaps` (0.76, with a warning), and:

* Terra receives **no VIIRS-equivalent FRP** (`NaN`, never 0 and never 1x). Its raw FRP and detections remain in the
  calendar (`terra_detections`, `terra_frp_mw`) and in `calendar_cells.csv`, with `scale_basis = none`.
* Aqua's k is **not** transferred to Terra. `PYRO_APPLY_UNVALIDATED_K=1` is an explicit opt-in that applies a
  low-trust fit or the best-validated satellite's k, and labels every affected value (`own_low_trust`,
  `transferred:<satellite>`) in `scale_basis` and in the console.
* The calendar's `modis_frp_viirs_equiv_mw` is therefore **Aqua only**, which is also the like-for-like comparison with
  S-NPP (same overpass time). Summing Terra and Aqua would mix two different diurnal samples.

### 1.3 Is k stable between years?

Per-year k (cluster-bootstrap, 0.5 degree cells) for Aqua:

| year | 2015 | 2016 | 2017 | 2018 | 2019 | 2020 | 2021 | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| k | 0.94 | 0.89 | 0.77 | 1.03 | 0.67 | 0.72 | 0.82 | 0.81 | 0.98 | 0.68 |
| SE of ln k | 0.10 | 0.07 | 0.08 | 0.12 | 0.13 | 0.14 | 0.18 | 0.31 | 0.13 | 0.15 |

(2025 has 25 matched pixels and is left out.) The largest minus the smallest year is **45 % of the median**, which is the
figure that raises the stability question. It is not, however, a valid stability statistic: the range grows with the
number of years and is set by the noisiest year, and the per-year standard errors are 7-31 %. `harmonize` therefore
asks the right question, *does the scatter exceed its sampling error?*, with a random-effects analysis of ln k
(`kstats.heterogeneity`, Cochran Q / I^2 / DerSimonian-Laird tau with a Q-profile interval):

| statistic | value |
|---|---|
| Cochran Q (9 df), p | 13.9, p = 0.13 |
| I^2 | 35 % |
| between-year SD tau | 8.6 % (95% interval 0 % - 24.8 %) |

Reading: the 45 % range is **compatible with sampling error alone**, so there is no evidence that k drifts. But with ten
noisy years the data **cannot exclude a real year-to-year variation of up to about 25 %**. The leave-one-year-out check
is judged against sampling error as well (Bonferroni-corrected over the years); no year fails it. `k_by_year.png`
shows this directly: every per-year interval overlaps the pooled interval.

Consequences in the code:

* **k_trust stays MEDIUM.** HIGH needs, besides >= 500 pixels, >= 30 days and >= 3 years, a bootstrap CI no wider than
  20 % of k (it is 20.3 %), no significant between-year heterogeneity, and a 95 % upper limit of tau of at most 15 %
  (`PYRO_K_TAU_MAX`; it is 24.8 %). The old rule (max-min spread <= 20 %) was replaced because it is the wrong
  statistic, not to make the grade easier: under the new rule the grade is the same, for stated reasons.
* **The uncertainty is carried forward.** `k_prediction_interval95_new_year` (0.65-1.03) combines the sampling error
  of the pooled k with tau (Higgins et al. 2009). It is applied to the calendar as
  `modis_frp_viirs_equiv_lo_mw` / `_hi_mw`. The plain CI (0.74-0.91) is the interval for the pooled constant, not for
  a given year.
* **Monte-Carlo noise cannot flip a grade.** The bootstrap uses 10,000 replicates (`PYRO_N_BOOT`). With 1,000 the CI
  width wandered between 19.4 % and 20.9 % across seeds, which straddles the 20 % threshold.

### 1.4 What else k depends on (`k_sensitivity` in `frp_mapping.json`)

A constant k is an approximation. Re-estimating it under every defensible alternative (same estimand) moves it by at
most 11.6 %:

| variant | n | k |
|---|---|---|
| primary (<= 30 min) | 1,864 | 0.821 |
| <= 10 min / 15 min / 60 min / 120 min | 838 / 1,060 / 2,986 / 3,245 | 0.749 / 0.782 / 0.858 / 0.880 |
| daytime / night-time pixels | 640 / 1,224 | 0.772 / 0.887 |
| presumed vegetation fires (type 0) / static sources (type 2) | 766 / 1,092 | 0.804 / 0.853 |
| MODIS FRP below / above the median | 941 / 923 | 0.916 / 0.796 |
| without the largest 1 % / 5 % of MODIS pixels | 1,845 / 1,770 | 0.886 / 0.908 |
| product version 6.03 / 61.03 | 1,640 / 224 | 0.828 / 0.789 |

k falls as MODIS FRP rises, differs by day / night, and grows with the allowed time gap. Also worth knowing: 59 % of the
matched pixels are static sources (flares, industry; 36 % of the FRP) at only 117 distinct locations: the 1,864 pixels come from
759 distinct 0.01-degree sites, so the effective sample is much smaller than 1,864, which is what the clustered
bootstrap accounts for.

### 1.5 k as a function of fire size, day/night and time gap (`src/kmodel.py`)

Section 1.4 shows that a constant k moves with fire size, day/night and the allowed time gap, but it varies one factor at
a time, and the factors are confounded (night-time pixels are smaller, so "night" partly measures "small"). The model
fits them jointly:

    VIIRS FRP in the footprint = MODIS FRP * k,    log k = a + s * log(MODIS FRP / f_ref) + c * night + d * dt_hours

* **Estimator.** Quasi-Poisson regression with a log link and `log(MODIS FRP)` as offset, fitted by IRLS, confidence
  intervals from the same 0.5-degree-cell cluster bootstrap as the constant k (2,000 replicates). It targets the
  conditional *mean*, which is what the calendar sums, so no retransformation correction is needed (a regression of
  `log(VIIRS)` on `log(MODIS)` targets the median pixel). With no covariate it equals `sum(VIIRS) / sum(MODIS)`, the
  constant k, and in-sample the fitted total equals the observed total exactly (both are tested in `selftest.py`).
  The log-log regression is reported as a cross-check (`log_log_ols`): its elasticity of 0.82 (CI 0.74-0.90) agrees with
  `1 + s = 0.86`.
* **Which terms are kept is decided by prediction, not by p-values.** All 8 subsets of {size, night, dt} are scored by the
  held-out Poisson deviance with whole cells held out (10 folds); the simplest subset within one paired standard error
  of the best is kept (`k_model.cv.candidates` has every score).

| terms | CV deviance | vs best (SE) | held-out total, predicted / observed |
|---|---|---|---|
| **size** | **22,503** | best | 1.001 |
| size + dt | 22,580 | +77 (140) | 1.001 |
| size + night | 22,648 | +145 (76) | 1.009 |
| size + night + dt | 22,686 | +184 (160) | 1.008 |
| dt / night + dt / night | 23,174 / 23,191 / 23,215 | +671 / +688 / +712 | 0.995 / 1.006 / 1.007 |
| none (constant k) | 23,227 | +724 (492) | 0.993 |

* **Result (Aqua x S-NPP, 1,864 pixels, 101 cells).** Only fire size is selected. Coefficient of log FRP -0.138
  (95 % CI -0.218 to -0.073): k changes by -27 % (CI -40 % to -15 %) for every 10x increase in MODIS FRP. In the model
  with all three terms, night is -0.09 (CI -0.26 to +0.12) and time gap +0.36 per hour (CI -0.28 to +1.10): neither can
  be told from zero, i.e. the day/night difference of section 1.4 (0.772 vs 0.887) is largely fire size, and the time-gap
  effect is not identified by pairs that are all within 30 minutes. Held-out deviance is 3.1 % below the constant k
  (3.8 % under leave-one-year-out).
* **What changes, out of fold, by quintile of MODIS FRP** (observed / predicted total):

| MODIS FRP (MW) | 3.6-7.6 | 7.7-9.9 | 10.0-13.2 | 13.3-20.7 | 20.8-1078 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| constant k = 0.821 | 1.22 | 1.11 | 1.07 | 1.09 | 0.93 |
| size model | 0.95 | 0.90 | 0.90 | 0.97 | 1.07 |

  The constant k under-predicts the smallest pixels by 22 % and over-predicts the largest by 7 %; the model cuts the
  worst bias to 10 %. It does **not** remove it: the middle bins stay ~10 % too high, a curvature the log-linear form
  does not capture. A quadratic term was tried: it lowers the held-out deviance by 1.4 % (1.2 standard errors), is not
  kept by the one-standard-error rule and is worse for the smallest pixels, so it is not used.
* **Applied at dt = 0.** The VIIRS-equivalent FRP is what VIIRS would have seen at the MODIS overpass; a MODIS pixel with no
  VIIRS pixel has no gap of its own. Fire size is clipped to the central 99.8 % of the fitted pixels (4.2-731 MW here)
  and the ratio is held at the boundary beyond that, because a log-linear trend is not trusted outside the data.
  Unknown day/night uses the fitted night share. Winsorising the size covariate steepens a perfectly log-linear
  slope slightly (simulation, true slope -0.300: estimated -0.3004 unclipped, -0.3019 at the default 0.1 % per tail,
  -0.3067 at 0.5 %), the price of not extrapolating from a few very large fires.
* **Interval.** `modis_frp_viirs_equiv_lo_mw` / `_hi_mw` keep the *relative* width of the constant-k prediction interval
  (sampling error of the aggregate scale and between-year tau, 0.65-1.03 around 0.82). Uncertainty of the size slope is
  in `k_model.coefficients_*` and the band of `k_model.png`, but it is not added to the interval: it mostly cancels in
  daily totals, which this work did not verify.
* **Effect on the calendar.** The 11-year sum of `modis_frp_viirs_equiv_mw` falls by 1.0 %; the applied aggregate scale
  is 0.813 (constant 0.821). Individual days move more, in either direction, depending on the size mix.
  The anomaly series (VIIRS only), `k`, its CI and `k_trust` are unchanged; `PYRO_K_MODEL=0` reproduces the previous
  outputs exactly.
* **Validated on known answers (`selftest.py`).** Constant truth: nothing is adopted. Planted truth (size exponent 0.7,
  night x 0.75, gap decay 0.8 per hour): the three coefficients are recovered (-0.295 / -0.289 / -0.69 against
  -0.300 / -0.288 / -0.80, CIs cover), all three terms are selected and the out-of-fold bias by size bin falls from 75 % to
  2 %. Over 24 random seeds the 95 % intervals covered the planted values 92-96 % of the time.
* **Not done.** The model is conditional on a MODIS detection like the constant k, fitted on Egypt only (59 % of the
  pixels are static sources at ~117 locations; holding out whole cells limits, but cannot remove, the advantage of having
  seen those sites), and it says nothing about the diurnal cycle: Terra, ~150 minutes from S-NPP, still gets no scale.

## 2. Observation status: a day without observations is not a day without fire

### 2.1 What changed in the calendar

`calendar_daily.csv` used to write `0.0` as the harmonized value on days that were not observed (11 days), and a day
absent from the VIIRS file could be silently filled with a MODIS-scaled value. Both are removed.

* Every sensor-level number (`viirs_*`, `aqua_*`, `terra_*`, `modis_*`) is **NaN** on a day that sensor did not deliver
  data, and 0 only when it was observed and detected nothing.
* `harmonized_status` says exactly what a row is:

| `harmonized_status` | meaning | `harmonized_frp_mw_viirs_equiv` | days |
|---|---|---|---|
| `measured` | VIIRS reference observed, >= 1 detection | VIIRS FRP | 3,944 |
| `measured_zero` | VIIRS observed, 0 detections, in a sparse series where that is plausible | 0.0 | 0 |
| `measured_low_coverage` | VIIRS present but the day lost overpasses (outage suspect): the number is a **lower bound** | VIIRS FRP, flagged | 35 |
| `observed_obscured` | VIIRS delivered data, 0 detections, but the clear-sky share is below `PYRO_CLOUD_MIN_CLEAR` (needs a cloud file, section 2.5) | NaN | 0 (no cloud file) |
| `viirs_not_observed` | no usable VIIRS data (absent from the raw file) | NaN | 36 |
| `partial_day` | the window starts / ends inside this UTC day | NaN | 2 |

  `harmonized_is_measured` is true for the first three. `harmonized_source` is `VIIRS <satellite>` or `none`; it is never MODIS.
* Separate per-source flags (`viirs_observed`, `aqua_observed`, `terra_observed`, `modis_observed`) replace the single
  `day_observed`, which is kept for continuity.

### 2.2 How "not observed" is inferred

A day is missing, not quiet, when an empty day is implausible for that file at that time (`kstats.infer_missing_days`).
The days around a run of empty days give a negative-binomial model of an ordinary day, and the run is a gap when
`P(0 rows)^length < 1e-4`. For a dense file (S-NPP over Egypt, ~90 rows a day) **one** empty day is a gap; for a sparse
file (Aqua, 1.7 rows a day, 47 % of days empty) only long runs are. The test is run **per satellite**
(`raw_rows_per_day_by_satellite` in the coverage files), so an outage of one satellite is not hidden by another's rows.
The earlier rule used a smoothed share of empty days and missed single-day gaps in dense files; 2024-09-26 was classified
as "observed, zero fire" and then filled from MODIS. It now finds all 36 days that are absent from the VIIRS file, and
one 20-day Aqua run (2020-08-16) plus two Terra runs (9 and 13 days). These flags are inferred from row counts, not read from a satellite status log.

### 2.3 Partial outages (`measured_low_coverage`)

A day that lost overpasses is biased low and would later be flagged as a "low" anomaly. A day is flagged when the local
(+-15 day) median is at least 20 detections a day and either it has <= 1 overpass cluster and fewer than 50 % of the
local median (`PYRO_COV_MIN_FRAC`), or fewer than 20 % of it (`PYRO_COV_EXTREME_FRAC`; reached by under 1 % of days).
`viirs_overpass_clusters` and `viirs_expected_detections` are written to the calendar. A sparse series cannot reveal an
outage this way, so nothing is flagged there. 35 days are flagged here, e.g. 2018-02-11 (4 detections against ~67
expected) and 2022-07-26 (7 against 74, the day before a 15-day gap).

### 2.4 MODIS is not used to fill VIIRS gaps

The old fallback (`MODIS scaled`) is gone. On the 3,959 days where VIIRS was measured and Aqua observed, the k-scaled Aqua
daily total reproduces the VIIRS total poorly: **VIIRS totals are 9.2 times the scaled Aqua totals** (median daily
ratio 9.3), Aqua has no detection on 46 % of days, and the log-log correlation is 0.34 (R^2 = 0.12) (`daily_total_relationship`). k aligns FRP
inside co-detected footprints only; it does not recover the small fires MODIS misses. A filled day would look like a
collapse of fire activity. Days with no VIIRS data but Aqua detections are marked `modis_only_evidence = true` and
`modis_frp_viirs_equiv_mw` stays available as information, never as the series.

### 2.5 Cloud-obscured days (`observed_obscured`, optional; `src/cloud_mask.py`)

Sections 2.2 and 2.3 separate "the sensor did not look" from "no fire". A third case remained: the satellite passed, the
file has rows for the day, and cloud hid the ground, so 0 detections says little about fire. FIRMS files contain no
cloud information, so such a day was a `measured_zero` (or, in a dense series, `measured_low_coverage`).

* **Input.** `PYRO_CLOUD_FILE`: a CSV with one row per UTC day (and optionally per satellite): `date` and `clear_fraction`
  (share of the area of interest that was clear, 0-1; `cloud_fraction`, `clear_percent`, `cloud_percent` are accepted and
  never guessed: a value above 1 in a fraction column is an error). Several rows for one day are averaged. A satellite
  with rows of its own uses only those; otherwise rows without a satellite apply to the reference VIIRS satellite.
  The file is validated before the slow steps; a bad or unreadable file stops the run, because a cloud file that is
  silently ignored would look like clear sky.
* **Rule.** On a day that is complete and observed, with clear share `c` and `PYRO_CLOUD_MIN_CLEAR` = 0.5 by default:

| situation | `harmonized_status` |
|---|---|
| not complete / not observed | `partial_day` / `viirs_not_observed` (unchanged; cloud cannot matter) |
| `c < 0.5`, 0 detections | `observed_obscured`, harmonized value NaN |
| `c < 0.5`, detections, `PYRO_CLOUD_OBSCURE_NONZERO=1` | `observed_obscured`, harmonized value NaN (the sensor's own FRP stays in `viirs_frp_mw`) |
| `c < 0.5`, detections (default) | unchanged (`measured` / `measured_low_coverage`), flagged `viirs_cloud_limited` |
| `c >= 0.5`, or cloud cover unknown | unchanged: an empty day is a measured zero / outage suspect as before |

* **Unknown is not clear.** A day missing from the file, or with an empty value, has unknown cloud cover and keeps its old
  status; `viirs_clear_fraction` is NaN there and the count of such days is written to `frp_mapping.json`
  (`cloud_obscuration.days_without_cloud_info`).
* **Cloud wins over the outage detector.** An empty day under cloud is expected; an empty day under clear sky in a dense
  series is still `measured_low_coverage`.
* **Detections are real.** A day with detections is not turned into a missing day by default: its FRP is a lower bound,
  as for `measured_low_coverage`, and it is only flagged. Switch on `PYRO_CLOUD_OBSCURE_NONZERO` to drop those days from
  the anomaly series as well (this also removes real events that were seen through gaps in the cloud).
* **Anomalies.** `observed_obscured` days are never scored (`anomaly_report.json`: `cloud_obscured_days_excluded`). In a
  sparse series this removes cloud-caused zeros from the baseline, which therefore moves up slightly and no longer
  makes the next clear day look anomalous.
* **What it does not do.** The clear share is one number per day for the whole area of interest: 60 % clear over a large
  area can still hide the one region that burns, and the threshold 0.5 is a convention, not an estimate. The cloud
  product (MOD35 / MYD35, a VIIRS cloud mask) is not downloaded by this code, and how "probably clear" pixels are counted
  and day and night overpasses are averaged is decided when the file is built (`cloud_mask.clear_share_from_mod35`
  counts confident and probably clear pixels as clear, and pixels where the mask is undetermined as not clear). Only the
  VIIRS reference series is affected; the Aqua and Terra columns are not changed. The Egypt results in this document were
  produced without a cloud file and are unchanged.

## 3. Validation of the anomaly episodes

An episode (`anomaly_events.csv`) is a run of >= 3 elevated days (robust z >= 2, gaps <= 2 days merged) with a peak z >= 3.
That is a statistical candidate, not a demonstration that the event was exceptional.

### 3.1 What was already robust, and what was not

The production z already used a **median / MAD** baseline (not mean / SD), a **same-season window** (+-7 days) in **all
other years** (leave-one-year-out). Missing days were already NaN. What was missing was evidence that these choices matter or
do not, and the checks listed in the review. They are in `anomaly_validation.py` and run by default (about 25 s;
`PYRO_ANOM_VALIDATE=0` skips them).

### 3.2 Results

| Question | Test | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Is the seasonal cycle removed? | median z and robust SD of z by month; negative control without a seasonal baseline | monthly median z within +-0.04, robust SD 0.99-1.10. The control flags 62 % of its high days in the three peak-FRP months against 35 % for the production baseline, and finds 5 instead of 4 episodes |
| Do extreme observations distort the baseline? | classical SD vs robust sigma of the same-season pools | SD / robust sigma 1.04 (median), 1.15 (90th percentile); mean - median -0.03 sigma. The distortion is small, but the classical z finds only 2 of the 4 episodes (it is less sensitive) |
| Do missing observations create artificial lows? | re-admit the outage-suspect days; low flags next to gaps; random 10 % / 25 % loss; three 15-day outages a year | with the 35 suspect days kept there would be 36 low flags, **23 of them (64 %) on those days**; 16 remain, none next to an unobserved day, none on a genuine zero day. High flags are unchanged (20 vs 20). Random data loss creates a new episode in 7-30 % of records |
| Does the baseline period matter? | windows of +-3 / 14 / 21 days; earlier / later half of the years; neighbouring years skipped; earlier years only; annual level removed | yes, for one episode (below) |
| Do the same episodes appear under a robust alternative? | IQR scale; distribution-free position of the peak among same-season values of other years | see table |

`annual_level_trend`: the yearly median level of the series **falls 5.6 % per year** (Kendall tau = -0.78, p = 0.0003).
A leave-one-year-out baseline is therefore too low for the early years and too high for the late years, the reason the
"2015-2019 only" baseline produces 118 low days. Removing the annual level is offered as `PYRO_ANOM_DETREND=1` (off by
default so the baseline stays comparable with earlier runs); the validation always evaluates it as a variant.

### 3.3 The four episodes

| | dates | peak z | consensus variants detecting it | random-loss replicates | classical z | peak vs same-season values | family-wise p (heavy-tailed / Gaussian null) | robustness |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | 2016-09-27..10-02 | 3.25 | 3 / 8 | 69 % | no | new maximum | 0.14 / 0.05 | FRAGILE |
| E2 | 2022-06-23..06-27 | 3.78 | 9 / 9 | 100 % | yes | new maximum | 0.15 / 0.02 | ROBUST |
| E3 | 2023-06-17..06-21 | 3.69 | 8 / 9 | 91 % | no | 98th percentile | 0.17 / 0.03 | ROBUST |
| E4 | 2023-10-19..10-22 | 3.56 | 6 / 9 | 70 % | no | new maximum | 0.31 / 0.07 | MODERATE |

`robustness` = the lower of the share of consensus variants and of random-loss replicates that still detect the episode
(>= 0.8 ROBUST, >= 0.5 MODERATE, else FRAGILE). A variant only counts for an episode if its baseline holds >= 45 values.
The classical z, the re-admission of outage days and the no-season control are reported but not counted. E1 is in 2016,
where the declining trend makes the baseline too low, and it disappears when the annual level is removed.

Against the null model (section 3.4), **none of the four is individually exceptional**, and as a group they are
borderline: four episodes in 11 years against 1.3 expected under the heavy-tailed null (p = 0.051; Gaussian null 0.6
expected, p = 0.005). Individual high days behave the same way: 20 days with z >= 3 against 17 expected under the
heavy-tailed null fitted to the non-event days.

### 3.4 The null model

`null_calibration` estimates what ordinary variability produces, from the **non-event days only** (episodes +-3 days
removed, so they do not define their own null): a Gaussian and a Student-t (df 13) marginal and the lag-1 autocorrelation
of the non-event days (0.31), simulated as 2,000 records with the real validity pattern and run through the same episode
rule. The family-wise p of an episode is the share of null records containing an episode at least as long and as high.
This is a screen, not a significance test: it is stationary, has one lag of memory and no knowledge of sensor,
algorithm, land-cover or fuel changes. FRP alone cannot test those.

### 3.5 HotSync-style active-cell anomaly score

`src.hotsync_anomaly` adds a second, count-based signal beside the primary FRP anomaly detector. It is run automatically
by `python -m src.anomaly` and does not change `anomalies_daily.csv`'s existing FRP `z` or the validated FRP episodes.

The activity metric is the daily number of active grid cells. On complete, valid VIIRS days where MODIS was also observed,
it uses the sensor union `MODIS cells + VIIRS cells - cells active in both`. When MODIS is not observed but VIIRS is valid,
it converts the VIIRS cell count to the union scale using the ratio of summed union counts to summed VIIRS counts on
at least `PYRO_HOTSYNC_ANOM_MIN_OVERLAP_DAYS` (default 30) overlap days. If that calibration is unavailable, it uses
VIIRS counts alone for the whole run instead of mixing incompatible scales. **MODIS-only days are never scored**; the
VIIRS validity mask, including outage and cloud exclusions, remains authoritative.

For each target day, the baseline is the mean activity within +/- `PYRO_HOTSYNC_ANOM_WINDOW` days of the same day of
year in all other years, with at least `PYRO_HOTSYNC_ANOM_MIN_POOL` values. The HotSync Z-score is

`z_activity = (observed - seasonal_mean) / max(seasonal_population_sd, 1 + sqrt(seasonal_mean))`.

A day is flagged high at `PYRO_HOTSYNC_ANOM_Z` (default 2.5) and only scored when activity is at least
`PYRO_HOTSYNC_ANOM_MIN_ACTIVITY` (default 5). This is a mean/SD score, not the robust median/MAD score used for FRP.

The daily file gains `hotsync_activity_count`, `hotsync_activity_baseline`, `hotsync_activity_ratio`,
`hotsync_activity_z`, `hotsync_activity_flag` and `hotsync_activity_source`. Additional outputs are
`hotsync_activity_anomalies.csv` (up to eight strongest, non-neighbouring days) and
`hotsync_activity_overview.png`. The method uses PyroScope's `PYRO_CELL_DEG` grid (default 0.5 degrees), not
HotSync's ~2 km grid, so numerical activity counts are not directly comparable between the applications.

## 4. Spatial resolution and the 0.5 degree grid

### 4.1 Where a grid is used, and where it is not

Native resolution is used for **everything that determines FRP**: matching is footprint-aware (a VIIRS 375 m pixel is
assigned to the MODIS pixel footprint it falls in), k is a ratio of pixel sums, and every daily FRP total in the calendar
is a sum over detections. **None of these depends on the grid**, which is checked: k is identical (0.821) for every grid
in the sweep. The 0.5 degree grid (`PYRO_CELL_DEG`) is used for four things only:

1. the clusters of the bootstrap of k (neighbouring pixels of one fire are not independent);
2. the active-cell counts and the MODIS / VIIRS cell overlap (`modis_active_cells`, `viirs_active_cells`, `cells_active_in_both`);
3. the cell-level correlations in `frp_mapping.json`;
4. the cell-day table `calendar_cells.csv`, meant for filtering to an area of interest.

### 4.2 Sensitivity (`grid_sensitivity.csv`, `grid_sensitivity.png`; Aqua x S-NPP, 3,959 days)

| 0.05 | 27 | 24.7 % | 487 | 18.4 % | 38.9 | 64 % | 1.8 | 0.67 | 0.81 |

The bootstrap CI of k with other resampling units is 18.8 % for pixels, 18.7 % for days, 21.7 % for 0.01-degree sites and
19.4-20.3 % for 0.25-0.5 degree cells (`k_ci_by_cluster_unit`).

### 4.3 Why 0.5 degrees

1. **It does not split one fire.** The same fire seen by both sensors lies ~1 km apart in the two products; at 0.5 degrees
   a pair of detections that far apart falls in different cells 2.5 % of the time, at 0.05 degrees 25 %.
2. **It is where the bootstrap CI of k is widest.** Clustering at 0.25-0.5 degrees captures the spatial dependence between
   pixels (the CI is wider than the 18.8 % of the unclustered bootstrap) while leaving 100-170 clusters. Larger cells leave too few clusters
   (< 30) and the percentile bootstrap collapses to a falsely narrow interval (12.9 % at 2 degrees).
3. **It matches the standard.** The MODIS Climate Modeling Grid fire products are also 0.5 degrees (Giglio et al. 2006), so
   cell-level numbers stay comparable with that literature.
4. **It resolves the regions of interest.** The Nile Delta and Valley, the Sinai, the Red Sea coast and the main
   flaring areas fall in different cells.

It is a choice, not a physical scale: 0.25 and 0.5 degrees are indistinguishable for the CI of k, and the cell-level
metrics move smoothly with the size.

### 4.4 What is lost, and how to read the cell metrics

* **Sub-cell position** (+-28 km) and the **number of distinct fires**: several fires in a cell on one day count as one
  active cell (VIIRS detections per active cell grow from 1.8 to 7.3 between 0.05 and 2 degrees). VIIRS / MODIS
  *active-cell* ratios and overlap shares are properties of the grid; quote them with `cell_deg`, never as sensor
  properties (the ratio of detections, 55.7 for Aqua, does not depend on it).
* **Spatial heterogeneity inside a cell**: a flare field and an agricultural area in one cell are merged.
* **Correlations inflate with aggregation** (modifiable areal unit problem): r(log) of per-cell totals over the whole record
  rises from 0.81 to 0.98 with cell size; the fairer cell-day correlation is 0.67-0.74. The earlier README quoted the
  0.94 of the totals; `cell_day_corr_log` is reported next to it (0.69).
* **A lat/lon cell is not equal-area**: 0.5 x 0.5 degrees is 2,664 km^2 at the median latitude but 3,091 km^2 at the equator
  and about 1,550 km^2 at 60 degrees. Across Egypt (22-32 N) a cell is 9 % smaller at the northern edge than at the southern one (2,621 vs 2,866 km^2); `calendar_cells.csv`
  carries `cell_area_km2` for density normalisation. For a **global** run use an equal-area grid (not implemented).

`PYRO_GRID_SENS` changes the grids of the sweep (default 0.05, 0.1, 0.25, 0.5, 1, 2).

## 5. Known limits that this work does not remove

* k is conditional on a MODIS detection, depends on fire size (sections 1.4 and 1.5: the applied scale models this, the
  headline constant does not; ~10 % residual bias remains in mid-sized pixels) and has not been tested outside Egypt.
* Terra and any satellite without close-in-time matches with the reference cannot be harmonized by this method. A route
  that does not need close overpasses (e.g. through a third satellite or the polar overlaps of Terra and Aqua) is open.
  A diurnal-cycle correction is not implemented: Terra and Aqua each sit at one gap to S-NPP, so the shape of the
  FRP-versus-time-of-day curve is not identifiable from these pairs and would have to come from an independent source.
* Aqua's orbit drifts from ~13.2 h to ~14.4 h local time after 2022, so close matches become rarer; 2025 has too few pixels
  for a yearly k.
* The anomaly baseline mixes years of different activity (section 3.2). Episodes remain statistical candidates;
  confirmation needs independent evidence (burned-area, news, field reports).
* `*_observed` flags are inferred from row counts. Cloud obscuration (section 2.5) needs an external daily clear-sky
  file; without one, an empty observed day under cloud is still read as a measured zero.
* The seasonal window, MAD floor and episode rule are conventions, not estimates. The null model of section 3.4 is crude.

## 6. External burned-area validation (optional; `src/external_validation.py`)

The anomaly episodes in `anomaly_events.csv` are statistical candidates. Edit 7 adds an optional outcome check against
MODIS MCD64A1 Collection 6.1, a monthly 500 m burned-area product. Run it after `harmonize` and `anomaly`:

```bash
python -m src.external_validation --mcd64-dir /path/to/MCD64A1
```

By default it reads `anomaly_events.csv` and `calendar_cells.csv` from the configured processed directory and writes
`external_validation.csv` / `external_validation.json`. The event footprint is the set of VIIRS `cell_id` values
present in `calendar_cells.csv` between the episode's `start` and `end` dates. The requested `--cell-deg` must match
the `PYRO_CELL_DEG` used to build that calendar. The tool does not use bounding boxes from the event CSV; those columns
do not exist in the current Phase C event schema.

### 6.1 Date, geometry and area

MCD64A1 filenames encode the first day of the month as `AYYYYDDD` (for example, `A2020032` is 1 February 2020),
not as an approximate 30-day month number. The code selects Collection 6.1 files by year, month and sinusoidal `hXXvYY`
tile; it reads the named `Burn Date` and `QA` subdatasets (and `First Day` / `Last Day` when present). It evaluates every
month touched by an episode and filters `Burn Date` to the exact event date interval within that month. Positive burn
pixels are counted only where QA identifies land with sufficient valid data. Burned area uses the source raster's pixel
transform and CRS units, not a hard-coded 0.25 km² pixel assumption.

The spatial comparison is performed on the PyroScope event cells. Raster pixel centres are transformed from the
MCD64A1 projection to longitude/latitude and assigned to the same `cell_id` convention used by `harmonize.py`. The
reported overlap is the fraction of event VIIRS cells that contain at least one MCD64A1 burn pixel. This is explicitly
a grid-level spatial agreement metric; it is not a pixel-level match of individual active-fire detections to burned
pixels and depends on `PYRO_CELL_DEG`.

### 6.2 QA, coverage and the seasonal reference

For MCD64A1, QA bit 0 denotes land/water, bit 1 indicates sufficient valid data, and bit 2 flags a shortened mapping
period. Bits 5–7 encode special conditions for some unburned pixels. For a positive burned-area finding, a pixel must
be land, have the valid-data flag, have a valid positive `Burn Date`, and fall within the event dates. For a negative
finding, the pixel must additionally have reliable temporal coverage of the entire event segment (bit 2 clear, or
`First Day` / `Last Day` spanning the requested dates) and no unburned special-condition code. Missing monthly tile
files are counted separately. The tool reports the fraction of land-pixel observations that meet these stricter
coverage requirements.

The seasonal reference repeats the same month/day window in each other year available in the local MCD64A1 archive,
excluding the event's start year. Only reference periods with no missing expected tiles and at least the requested
valid coverage (`--min-coverage`, default 0.80) contribute to the baseline. The report gives the median, 90th
percentile, ratio to the median (undefined when the median is zero) and empirical percentile. A small or unavailable
reference sample is reported as such; it is not replaced by zero. `burned_area_km2` is populated only when the event
period has adequate complete coverage; `burned_area_observed_km2` is a separate partial subtotal, and must not be read as
the total burned area when `coverage_complete` is false.

Status meanings:

* `BURNED_AREA_DETECTED`: one or more QA-valid burned pixels overlap the event dates and event cells, with sufficient
  overall coverage.
* `BURNED_AREA_DETECTED_PARTIAL_COVERAGE`: positive burn evidence exists, but the full footprint is not adequately
  covered.
* `NO_BURN_IN_VALID_COVERAGE`: no burn was mapped and the full expected tiles / valid spatial-temporal coverage meet
  the threshold. This is a negative burned-area check, not proof that no active fire occurred.
* `MCD64_FILES_MISSING`: one or more expected tile/month granules are absent; this must not be interpreted as no burn.
* `INCONCLUSIVE_LOW_COVERAGE`: observations exist but do not provide enough valid coverage to confirm a no-burn result.
* `NO_VIIRS_EVENT_CELLS`: the event has no VIIRS cell rows in the supplied calendar for its date range.

### 6.3 Interpretation and limitations

MCD64A1 burn date is an approximate date derived from a monthly reflectance time series, not an active-fire detection
or an FRP measurement. A real event can have active-fire detections without a mapped burn scar (small, short-lived,
cloud-obscured or non-vegetation fires are examples), and a mapped burned pixel need not correspond one-to-one with a
FIRMS detection. MCD64A1 is a separate burned-area product, but it is based on MODIS observations and therefore is not
a fully independent sensor family from MODIS inputs elsewhere in PyroScope. The validation is optional, does not
change the anomaly score, and does not run automatically as part of `src.anomaly`.

The HDF reader requires Rasterio and PyProj, with HDF4 support in the linked GDAL build. These are optional runtime
dependencies and are intentionally not added to the core `requirements.txt`. `python -m src.selftest` tests the
filename/date parsing, QA masking, area arithmetic, coverage classification and missing-data behavior using synthetic
arrays; it does not validate an actual HDF driver or any downloaded MCD64A1 granule. Product details and QA semantics
are documented in the [MCD64A1 Collection 6.1 User's Guide](https://lpdaac.usgs.gov/documents/1006/MCD64_User_Guide_V61.pdf)
and the [LAADS MCD64A1 file specification](https://ladsweb.modaps.eosdis.nasa.gov/filespec/MODIS/61/MCD64A1/).

## 7. Out-of-core processing for large archives (Edit 8)

The original large-run failure mode had two parts: the raw preprocessing path retained every historical deduplication hash in a NumPy array, and `harmonize.py` loaded the complete MODIS and VIIRS standardized CSVs (plus matched-pair and cell/day tables) into pandas. Edit 8 adds an optional disk-backed route without changing the normal CLI or requiring Dask.

### 7.1 Dispatch and ingestion

`PYRO_OUT_OF_CORE=auto` is the default. `harmonize.py` estimates the combined standardized row count from a 1 MiB sample of each CSV and switches to `src.harmonize_streaming` at `PYRO_OUT_OF_CORE_ROWS` (default 3,000,000). `PYRO_OUT_OF_CORE=1` forces the disk-backed path; `PYRO_OUT_OF_CORE=0` forces the legacy in-memory reference path. The threshold is a dispatch heuristic, not a hard memory guarantee.

`preprocess.py` already reads FIRMS sources in `PYRO_CHUNK` row batches. Edit 8 keeps the same filtering and output schema, but stores cross-chunk duplicate keys in a temporary SQLite table instead of retaining an ever-growing in-memory hash array. Daily coverage counts remain in a compact day/satellite dictionary; the standardized CSVs and `daily_detection_counts.csv` retain their existing filenames and schema. `PYRO_DEDUPE=0` still disables cross-file deduplication explicitly.

### 7.2 Disk-backed harmonization

The streaming harmonizer stages standardized files into temporary SQLite tables in `PYRO_STREAM_CHUNK_ROWS` batches (default `PYRO_CHUNK`). It indexes UTC timestamps, queries one UTC day at a time, and matches one MODIS satellite against one VIIRS satellite and its `MATCH_MAX_MIN` time window. Full matched-pair results are appended to temporary CSV spill files. The exact constant-k numerator/denominator sufficient statistics are aggregated by year and spatial cell into SQLite; only the final per-cell array needed by the configured bootstrap is brought into memory. Per-year cell arrays are queried one year at a time, rather than retaining a Python dictionary for every year/cell combination.

After the scales are selected, detections are revisited one instrument/day at a time and aggregated to sensor/satellite/date/cell rows. Those cell/day aggregates are written to SQLite, not concatenated into one multi-year pandas frame. Daily totals, active-cell counts, saturation shares, overpass clusters, and cross-sensor cell overlap are calculated with SQL queries over these aggregates. `calendar_cells.csv` is exported with a bounded pandas chunk size; the daily calendar is built from full aggregates. The existing CSV filenames and principal columns are preserved, so Edit 7 and the anomaly pipeline continue to consume the same artifacts.

### 7.3 Exact versus sampled calculations

The headline constant `k`, its cell-cluster bootstrap interval, full matched-pair counts and year-by-cell summaries use all qualifying close-in-time pairs. This is not a sample estimate. Conditional-model fitting and pixel-level diagnostics necessarily require pixel rows in pandas; in out-of-core mode they use a deterministic bounded sample (`PYRO_STREAM_SAMPLE_ROWS`, default 100,000). Grid diagnostics use bounded raw-pixel samples (`PYRO_STREAM_DIAGNOSTIC_ROWS`, default 150,000 per sensor). Cluster-unit sensitivity uses at most 10,000 sampled pairs and at most 200 bootstrap replicates. `frp_mapping.json` records these caps and labels the sample-based quantities. `calendar_daily.csv` and `calendar_cells.csv` are generated from the full staged data, not the diagnostic sample.

### 7.4 Resource limits and interpretation

This is out-of-core processing, not infinite-memory processing. It needs substantial temporary disk space for SQLite tables/indexes, matched-pair spill files and outputs; the required space depends on the source archive and the number of matched pairs. Temporary staging files live under the configured processed directory and are removed when the run completes. Matching still loads one UTC day for one MODIS satellite and the matching VIIRS time window into memory. A single extraordinarily dense day can therefore remain a RAM bottleneck; exceptionally large one-day workloads should be pre-partitioned geographically or temporally. A very fine global `PYRO_CELL_DEG` also increases the number of spatial bootstrap clusters.

`plot_overview.py` already scans standardized CSVs by chunk, and the anomaly stage works on the much smaller daily calendar. Optional burned-area validation now scans the potentially large cell calendar in chunks when it constructs an event footprint. No new mandatory Python package is introduced. Synthetic tests compare streamed calendar outputs with the legacy calendar and test deterministic sampling and exact disk-backed `k` statistics; they cannot replace a representative full-archive benchmark on the user's hardware.
