# PyroScope methodology, validation and known limits

All numbers below come from the Egypt 2015-01-02 to 2025-12-31 standard-processing files shipped with this project
(MODIS Terra + Aqua, VIIRS S-NPP 375 m), run with the default settings. Every one of them is regenerated by
`python -m src.harmonize` and `python -m src.anomaly` and can be re-read from `frp_mapping.json`,
`grid_sensitivity.csv`, `anomaly_validation.json` and `anomaly_events.csv`.

| Review item | Where it is addressed |
|---|---|
| A. The harmonization factor is not stable enough; Aqua and Terra must be separated | section 1 |
| B. A day with no detections is not a day without observations | section 2 |
| C. The anomaly detection needs validation | section 3 |
| D. The 0.5 degree grid needs justification and a sensitivity analysis | section 4 |

## 1. The FRP scale k

### 1.1 What k is, and is not

`k = sum(VIIRS FRP inside MODIS footprints) / sum(MODIS FRP)`, computed over MODIS pixels that contain at least one
VIIRS 375 m detection, with the two overpasses at most 30 minutes apart (`PYRO_K_MAX_DT_MIN`). It is

* a ratio of **totals**, so it is dominated by the large fires, and it does not describe a typical pixel (the median
  of pixel ratios is 0.74 and the geometric mean 0.67, against 0.82 for the ratio of sums);
* **conditional on MODIS having detected the footprint**: it is not a detection-probability correction, and it says
  nothing about the many small fires only VIIRS sees;
* specific to **one MODIS satellite**: every satellite has its own orbit, overpass time and calibration history.

### 1.2 Aqua and Terra are separate

`fit_scale` is run once per MODIS satellite, each against the VIIRS reference satellite.

| | matched pixels within 30 min of S-NPP | k | status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Aqua | 1,864 | 0.821 (95% CI 0.739-0.905) | fitted; trust MEDIUM |
| Terra | 3 (median gap to S-NPP over all 1,288 pixel matches: 149 min) | none | NOT_VALIDATED |

Terra crosses Egypt at about 10:30 local solar time and S-NPP at about 13:30, so a Terra pixel and an S-NPP pixel in
the same place are three hours apart and see different fire states (the diurnal cycle of fires). A ratio computed
over those pairs is not a calibration. It is reported only as `k_diagnostic_all_gaps` (0.76, with a warning), and:

* Terra receives **no VIIRS-equivalent FRP** (`NaN`, never 0 and never 1x). Its raw FRP and detections remain in the
  calendar (`terra_detections`, `terra_frp_mw`) and in `calendar_cells.csv`, with `scale_basis = none`.
* Aqua's k is **not** transferred to Terra. `PYRO_APPLY_UNVALIDATED_K=1` is an explicit opt-in that applies a
  low-trust fit or the best-validated satellite's k, and labels every affected value (`own_low_trust`,
  `transferred:<satellite>`) in `scale_basis` and in the console.
* The calendar's `modis_frp_viirs_equiv_mw` is therefore **Aqua only**, which is also the like-for-like comparison with
  S-NPP (same overpass time). Summing Terra and Aqua would mix two different diurnal samples.

### 1.3 Is k stable between years?

Per-year k (cluster-bootstrap, 0.5 degree cells) for Aqua:

| year | 2015 | 2016 | 2017 | 2018 | 2019 | 2020 | 2021 | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| k | 0.94 | 0.89 | 0.77 | 1.03 | 0.67 | 0.72 | 0.82 | 0.81 | 0.98 | 0.68 |
| SE of ln k | 0.10 | 0.07 | 0.08 | 0.12 | 0.13 | 0.14 | 0.18 | 0.31 | 0.13 | 0.15 |

(2025 has 25 matched pixels and is left out.) The largest minus the smallest year is **45 % of the median**, which is the
figure that raises the stability question. It is not, however, a valid stability statistic: the range grows with the
number of years and is set by the noisiest year, and the per-year standard errors are 7-31 %. `harmonize` therefore
asks the right question, *does the scatter exceed its sampling error?*, with a random-effects analysis of ln k
(`kstats.heterogeneity`, Cochran Q / I^2 / DerSimonian-Laird tau with a Q-profile interval):

| statistic | value |
|---|---|
| Cochran Q (9 df), p | 13.9, p = 0.13 |
| I^2 | 35 % |
| between-year SD tau | 8.6 % (95% interval 0 % - 24.8 %) |

Reading: the 45 % range is **compatible with sampling error alone**, so there is no evidence that k drifts. But with ten
noisy years the data **cannot exclude a real year-to-year variation of up to about 25 %**. The leave-one-year-out check
is judged against sampling error as well (Bonferroni-corrected over the years); no year fails it. `k_by_year.png`
shows this directly: every per-year interval overlaps the pooled interval.

Consequences in the code:

* **k_trust stays MEDIUM.** HIGH needs, besides >= 500 pixels, >= 30 days and >= 3 years, a bootstrap CI no wider than
  20 % of k (it is 20.3 %), no significant between-year heterogeneity, and a 95 % upper limit of tau of at most 15 %
  (`PYRO_K_TAU_MAX`; it is 24.8 %). The old rule (max-min spread <= 20 %) was replaced because it is the wrong
  statistic, not to make the grade easier: under the new rule the grade is the same, for stated reasons.
* **The uncertainty is carried forward.** `k_prediction_interval95_new_year` (0.65-1.03) combines the sampling error
  of the pooled k with tau (Higgins et al. 2009). It is applied to the calendar as
  `modis_frp_viirs_equiv_lo_mw` / `_hi_mw`. The plain CI (0.74-0.91) is the interval for the pooled constant, not for
  a given year.
* **Monte-Carlo noise cannot flip a grade.** The bootstrap uses 10,000 replicates (`PYRO_N_BOOT`). With 1,000 the CI
  width wandered between 19.4 % and 20.9 % across seeds, which straddles the 20 % threshold.

### 1.4 What else k depends on (`k_sensitivity` in `frp_mapping.json`)

A constant k is an approximation. Re-estimating it under every defensible alternative (same estimand) moves it by at
most 11.6 %:

| variant | n | k |
|---|---|---|
| primary (<= 30 min) | 1,864 | 0.821 |
| <= 10 min / 15 min / 60 min / 120 min | 838 / 1,060 / 2,986 / 3,245 | 0.749 / 0.782 / 0.858 / 0.880 |
| daytime / night-time pixels | 640 / 1,224 | 0.772 / 0.887 |
| presumed vegetation fires (type 0) / static sources (type 2) | 766 / 1,092 | 0.804 / 0.853 |
| MODIS FRP below / above the median | 941 / 923 | 0.916 / 0.796 |
| without the largest 1 % / 5 % of MODIS pixels | 1,845 / 1,770 | 0.886 / 0.908 |
| product version 6.03 / 61.03 | 1,640 / 224 | 0.828 / 0.789 |

k falls as MODIS FRP rises, differs by day / night, and grows with the allowed time gap. Also worth knowing: 59 % of the
matched pixels are static sources (flares, industry; 36 % of the FRP) at only 117 distinct locations: the 1,864 pixels come from
759 distinct 0.01-degree sites, so the effective sample is much smaller than 1,864, which is what the clustered
bootstrap accounts for.

## 2. Observation status: a day without observations is not a day without fire

### 2.1 What changed in the calendar

`calendar_daily.csv` used to write `0.0` as the harmonized value on days that were not observed (11 days), and a day
absent from the VIIRS file could be silently filled with a MODIS-scaled value. Both are removed.

* Every sensor-level number (`viirs_*`, `aqua_*`, `terra_*`, `modis_*`) is **NaN** on a day that sensor did not deliver
  data, and 0 only when it was observed and detected nothing.
* `harmonized_status` says exactly what a row is:

| `harmonized_status` | meaning | `harmonized_frp_mw_viirs_equiv` | days |
|---|---|---|---|
| `measured` | VIIRS reference observed, >= 1 detection | VIIRS FRP | 3,944 |
| `measured_zero` | VIIRS observed, 0 detections, in a sparse series where that is plausible | 0.0 | 0 |
| `measured_low_coverage` | VIIRS present but the day lost overpasses (outage suspect): the number is a **lower bound** | VIIRS FRP, flagged | 35 |
| `viirs_not_observed` | no usable VIIRS data (absent from the raw file) | NaN | 36 |
| `partial_day` | the window starts / ends inside this UTC day | NaN | 2 |

  `harmonized_is_measured` is true for the first three. `harmonized_source` is `VIIRS <satellite>` or `none`; it is never MODIS.
* Separate per-source flags (`viirs_observed`, `aqua_observed`, `terra_observed`, `modis_observed`) replace the single
  `day_observed`, which is kept for continuity.

### 2.2 How "not observed" is inferred

A day is missing, not quiet, when an empty day is implausible for that file at that time (`kstats.infer_missing_days`).
The days around a run of empty days give a negative-binomial model of an ordinary day, and the run is a gap when
`P(0 rows)^length < 1e-4`. For a dense file (S-NPP over Egypt, ~90 rows a day) **one** empty day is a gap; for a sparse
file (Aqua, 1.7 rows a day, 47 % of days empty) only long runs are. The test is run **per satellite**
(`raw_rows_per_day_by_satellite` in the coverage files), so an outage of one satellite is not hidden by another's rows.
The earlier rule used a smoothed share of empty days and missed single-day gaps in dense files; 2024-09-26 was classified
as "observed, zero fire" and then filled from MODIS. It now finds all 36 days that are absent from the VIIRS file, and
one 20-day Aqua run (2020-08-16) plus two Terra runs (9 and 13 days). These flags are inferred from row counts, not read from a satellite status log.

### 2.3 Partial outages (`measured_low_coverage`)

A day that lost overpasses is biased low and would later be flagged as a "low" anomaly. A day is flagged when the local
(+-15 day) median is at least 20 detections a day and either it has <= 1 overpass cluster and fewer than 50 % of the
local median (`PYRO_COV_MIN_FRAC`), or fewer than 20 % of it (`PYRO_COV_EXTREME_FRAC`; reached by under 1 % of days).
`viirs_overpass_clusters` and `viirs_expected_detections` are written to the calendar. A sparse series cannot reveal an
outage this way, so nothing is flagged there. 35 days are flagged here, e.g. 2018-02-11 (4 detections against ~67
expected) and 2022-07-26 (7 against 74, the day before a 15-day gap).

### 2.4 MODIS is not used to fill VIIRS gaps

The old fallback (`MODIS scaled`) is gone. On the 3,959 days where VIIRS was measured and Aqua observed, the k-scaled Aqua
daily total reproduces the VIIRS total poorly: **VIIRS totals are 9.2 times the scaled Aqua totals** (median daily
ratio 9.3), Aqua has no detection on 46 % of days, and the log-log correlation is 0.34 (R^2 = 0.12) (`daily_total_relationship`). k aligns FRP
inside co-detected footprints only; it does not recover the small fires MODIS misses. A filled day would look like a
collapse of fire activity. Days with no VIIRS data but Aqua detections are marked `modis_only_evidence = true` and
`modis_frp_viirs_equiv_mw` stays available as information, never as the series.

## 3. Validation of the anomaly episodes

An episode (`anomaly_events.csv`) is a run of >= 3 elevated days (robust z >= 2, gaps <= 2 days merged) with a peak z >= 3.
That is a statistical candidate, not a demonstration that the event was exceptional.

### 3.1 What was already robust, and what was not

The production z already used a **median / MAD** baseline (not mean / SD), a **same-season window** (+-7 days) in **all
other years** (leave-one-year-out). Missing days were already NaN. What was missing was evidence that these choices matter or
do not, and the checks listed in the review. They are in `anomaly_validation.py` and run by default (about 25 s;
`PYRO_ANOM_VALIDATE=0` skips them).

### 3.2 Results

| Question | Test | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Is the seasonal cycle removed? | median z and robust SD of z by month; negative control without a seasonal baseline | monthly median z within +-0.04, robust SD 0.99-1.10. The control flags 62 % of its high days in the three peak-FRP months against 35 % for the production baseline, and finds 5 instead of 4 episodes |
| Do extreme observations distort the baseline? | classical SD vs robust sigma of the same-season pools | SD / robust sigma 1.04 (median), 1.15 (90th percentile); mean - median -0.03 sigma. The distortion is small, but the classical z finds only 2 of the 4 episodes (it is less sensitive) |
| Do missing observations create artificial lows? | re-admit the outage-suspect days; low flags next to gaps; random 10 % / 25 % loss; three 15-day outages a year | with the 35 suspect days kept there would be 36 low flags, **23 of them (64 %) on those days**; 16 remain, none next to an unobserved day, none on a genuine zero day. High flags are unchanged (20 vs 20). Random data loss creates a new episode in 7-30 % of records |
| Does the baseline period matter? | windows of +-3 / 14 / 21 days; earlier / later half of the years; neighbouring years skipped; earlier years only; annual level removed | yes, for one episode (below) |
| Do the same episodes appear under a robust alternative? | IQR scale; distribution-free position of the peak among same-season values of other years | see table |

`annual_level_trend`: the yearly median level of the series **falls 5.6 % per year** (Kendall tau = -0.78, p = 0.0003).
A leave-one-year-out baseline is therefore too low for the early years and too high for the late years, the reason the
"2015-2019 only" baseline produces 118 low days. Removing the annual level is offered as `PYRO_ANOM_DETREND=1` (off by
default so the baseline stays comparable with earlier runs); the validation always evaluates it as a variant.

### 3.3 The four episodes

| | dates | peak z | consensus variants detecting it | random-loss replicates | classical z | peak vs same-season values | family-wise p (heavy-tailed / Gaussian null) | robustness |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | 2016-09-27..10-02 | 3.25 | 3 / 8 | 69 % | no | new maximum | 0.14 / 0.05 | FRAGILE |
| E2 | 2022-06-23..06-27 | 3.78 | 9 / 9 | 100 % | yes | new maximum | 0.15 / 0.02 | ROBUST |
| E3 | 2023-06-17..06-21 | 3.69 | 8 / 9 | 91 % | no | 98th percentile | 0.17 / 0.03 | ROBUST |
| E4 | 2023-10-19..10-22 | 3.56 | 6 / 9 | 70 % | no | new maximum | 0.31 / 0.07 | MODERATE |

`robustness` = the lower of the share of consensus variants and of random-loss replicates that still detect the episode
(>= 0.8 ROBUST, >= 0.5 MODERATE, else FRAGILE). A variant only counts for an episode if its baseline holds >= 45 values.
The classical z, the re-admission of outage days and the no-season control are reported but not counted. E1 is in 2016,
where the declining trend makes the baseline too low, and it disappears when the annual level is removed.

Against the null model (section 3.4), **none of the four is individually exceptional**, and as a group they are
borderline: four episodes in 11 years against 1.3 expected under the heavy-tailed null (p = 0.051; Gaussian null 0.6
expected, p = 0.005). Individual high days behave the same way: 20 days with z >= 3 against 17 expected under the
heavy-tailed null fitted to the non-event days.

### 3.4 The null model

`null_calibration` estimates what ordinary variability produces, from the **non-event days only** (episodes +-3 days
removed, so they do not define their own null): a Gaussian and a Student-t (df 13) marginal and the lag-1 autocorrelation
of the non-event days (0.31), simulated as 2,000 records with the real validity pattern and run through the same episode
rule. The family-wise p of an episode is the share of null records containing an episode at least as long and as high.
This is a screen, not a significance test: it is stationary, has one lag of memory and no knowledge of sensor,
algorithm, land-cover or fuel changes. FRP alone cannot test those.

## 4. Spatial resolution and the 0.5 degree grid

### 4.1 Where a grid is used, and where it is not

Native resolution is used for **everything that determines FRP**: matching is footprint-aware (a VIIRS 375 m pixel is
assigned to the MODIS pixel footprint it falls in), k is a ratio of pixel sums, and every daily FRP total in the calendar
is a sum over detections. **None of these depends on the grid**, which is checked: k is identical (0.821) for every grid
in the sweep. The 0.5 degree grid (`PYRO_CELL_DEG`) is used for four things only:

1. the clusters of the bootstrap of k (neighbouring pixels of one fire are not independent);
2. the active-cell counts and the MODIS / VIIRS cell overlap (`modis_active_cells`, `viirs_active_cells`, `cells_active_in_both`);
3. the cell-level correlations in `frp_mapping.json`;
4. the cell-day table `calendar_cells.csv`, meant for filtering to an area of interest.

### 4.2 Sensitivity (`grid_sensitivity.csv`, `grid_sensitivity.png`; Aqua x S-NPP, 3,959 days)

| cell size | area at median latitude (km^2) | P(1 km pair split by a cell edge) | cells in the k fit | CI width of k | VIIRS / MODIS active cells | share of MODIS cells also VIIRS | VIIRS detections per active cell | r(log), cell x day | r(log), cell totals |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.05 | 27 | 24.7 % | 487 | 18.4 % | 38.9 | 64 % | 1.8 | 0.67 | 0.81 |
| 0.10 | 107 | 12.3 % | 348 | 18.9 % | 37.2 | 71 % | 2.0 | 0.67 | 0.86 |
| 0.25 | 666 | 4.9 % | 172 | 19.4 % | 33.2 | 79 % | 2.4 | 0.69 | 0.93 |
| **0.50** | **2,664** | **2.5 %** | **101** | **20.3 %** | **27.2** | **84 %** | **3.1** | **0.69** | **0.94** |
| 1.00 | 10,654 | 1.2 % | 56 | 16.3 % | 21.1 | 90 % | 4.2 | 0.69 | 0.96 |
| 2.00 | 42,616 | 0.6 % | 25 | 12.9 % (unreliable) | 14.3 | 95 % | 7.3 | 0.74 | 0.98 |

The bootstrap CI of k with other resampling units is 18.8 % for pixels, 18.7 % for days, 21.7 % for 0.01-degree sites and
19.4-20.3 % for 0.25-0.5 degree cells (`k_ci_by_cluster_unit`).

### 4.3 Why 0.5 degrees

1. **It does not split one fire.** The same fire seen by both sensors lies ~1 km apart in the two products; at 0.5 degrees
   a pair of detections that far apart falls in different cells 2.5 % of the time, at 0.05 degrees 25 %.
2. **It is where the bootstrap CI of k is widest.** Clustering at 0.25-0.5 degrees captures the spatial dependence between
   pixels (the CI is wider than the 18.8 % of the unclustered bootstrap) while leaving 100-170 clusters. Larger cells leave too few clusters
   (< 30) and the percentile bootstrap collapses to a falsely narrow interval (12.9 % at 2 degrees).
3. **It matches the standard.** The MODIS Climate Modeling Grid fire products are also 0.5 degrees (Giglio et al. 2006), so
   cell-level numbers stay comparable with that literature.
4. **It resolves the regions of interest.** The Nile Delta and Valley, the Sinai, the Red Sea coast and the main
   flaring areas fall in different cells.

It is a choice, not a physical scale: 0.25 and 0.5 degrees are indistinguishable for the CI of k, and the cell-level
metrics move smoothly with the size.

### 4.4 What is lost, and how to read the cell metrics

* **Sub-cell position** (+-28 km) and the **number of distinct fires**: several fires in a cell on one day count as one
  active cell (VIIRS detections per active cell grow from 1.8 to 7.3 between 0.05 and 2 degrees). VIIRS / MODIS
  *active-cell* ratios and overlap shares are properties of the grid; quote them with `cell_deg`, never as sensor
  properties (the ratio of detections, 55.7 for Aqua, does not depend on it).
* **Spatial heterogeneity inside a cell**: a flare field and an agricultural area in one cell are merged.
* **Correlations inflate with aggregation** (modifiable areal unit problem): r(log) of per-cell totals over the whole record
  rises from 0.81 to 0.98 with cell size; the fairer cell-day correlation is 0.67-0.74. The earlier README quoted the
  0.94 of the totals; `cell_day_corr_log` is reported next to it (0.69).
* **A lat/lon cell is not equal-area**: 0.5 x 0.5 degrees is 2,664 km^2 at the median latitude but 3,091 km^2 at the equator
  and about 1,550 km^2 at 60 degrees. Across Egypt (22-32 N) a cell is 9 % smaller at the northern edge than at the southern one (2,621 vs 2,866 km^2); `calendar_cells.csv`
  carries `cell_area_km2` for density normalisation. For a **global** run use an equal-area grid (not implemented).

`PYRO_GRID_SENS` changes the grids of the sweep (default 0.05, 0.1, 0.25, 0.5, 1, 2).

## 5. Known limits that this work does not remove

* k is conditional on a MODIS detection, depends on fire size (section 1.4) and has not been tested outside Egypt.
* Terra and any satellite without close-in-time matches with the reference cannot be harmonized by this method. A route
  that does not need close overpasses (e.g. through a third satellite or the polar overlaps of Terra and Aqua) is open.
* Aqua's orbit drifts from ~13.2 h to ~14.4 h local time after 2022, so close matches become rarer; 2025 has too few pixels
  for a yearly k.
* The anomaly baseline mixes years of different activity (section 3.2). Episodes remain statistical candidates;
  confirmation needs independent evidence (burned-area, news, field reports).
* `*_observed` flags are inferred from row counts.
* The seasonal window, MAD floor and episode rule are conventions, not estimates. The null model of section 3.4 is crude.
